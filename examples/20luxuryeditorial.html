Éclat has made clothes on the same two floors above a locksmith's on the rue de Charonne since 1978, and the collection for Autumn/Winter 2026 is the first since 2011 that has been cut entirely in the house. The fabrics arrived in January. Cutting began in March. Nothing was delegated, and nothing is a reissue.

The argument the collection is built around is a small one: that a city in November is neither warm nor cold, and that clothes designed for either are wrong on roughly half the days of the year. So the coats are unlined and the wool is heavy enough to stand on its own. The knits are loose at the shoulder and long at the wrist. Nothing is waterproof, because nothing in Paris is.

“We stopped designing for the season and started designing for the walk from the door to the taxi.” Inès Calloway, creative director

I

The first floor is for cutting.
The second is for everything else.

There are twenty-two people on the two floors. Eleven cutters, four fitters, two in the sample room, and the rest of us. The pattern room is air-conditioned to a degree that visitors consistently describe as hostile.

Cutting is done in one pass, on the full width of the cloth, because a coat cut in two pieces will never hang the same way as a coat cut in one. This is slower and it is the entire reason the collection looks the way it does.

Everything in the collection is cut, sewn and finished above the locksmith's. We have not sent a garment out of France since 2011 and we do not intend to start.

Lead time on a made-to-measure coat is eleven weeks. We will tell you that at the fitting, not after.
