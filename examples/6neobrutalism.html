How it works

Three steps and one rule: if it does not fit on the board, it is not this week.

  1. 1

    Make the grid

    Five columns, Monday to Friday, or whatever your week actually looks like. Add a sixth column for things that take all week. That is the entire data model.

  2. 2

    Drop the work in

    Every card has an owner, a size, and a due day. The workload column updates as you drag, so overloading a person is visible before it becomes a resignation.

  3. 3

    Close the week

    Friday afternoon, the board becomes a two-minute review: what moved, what did not, what is coming back. Old weeks stay readable, and nobody needs a status document.

Questions

The ones people actually ask

What happens to my data if I cancel?

Nothing, and that is the point. The board is a file you export, and Flockup hands you the whole thing as JSON or CSV on the cancellation screen. There is no export fee, no retention clause and no “contact support to retrieve your work”.

Is this another thing to maintain every day?

No. The intended cadence is one board update a week, usually under ten minutes. There is no inbox, no request queue and no notification centre — if something needs you, it sits on the board in your name.

Can clients see it without an account?

Yes. A guest link opens a single card or column read-only in any browser. Guests cannot list boards, cannot search, and the link can be revoked from the same screen you created it.

Why is there no chat?

Because a chat thread attached to a card is a planning tool with worse ergonomics. If a decision happens in a conversation, write one line on the card. The card is the record, the chat is the noise.

Do you have a plan for 200 people?

Not really — at that size you need something with actual resource scheduling, and we would rather say so. Teams between 3 and 30 are what we are good at, and that is the entire product.
