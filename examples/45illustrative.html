Parcelly picks up from your door, sorts it overnight and delivers across the country. This page explains exactly what happens in between — the boring parts included, because the boring parts are where parcels go wrong.

How sending a parcel works, step by step

1

Collection, and a photo of the box

Choose a slot and our courier walks up to your door with a scanner. They'll photograph the parcel and the label together before it goes in the bag — that photo is what we use if it goes missing, so it matters.

Collection is free on orders over £20. Under that, £1.50.

2

Overnight sort at the depot

Parcels reach our two depots that night and come off the belt by 4am. A human reads the label; the machine only reads the barcode. Anything hand-written or damaged goes in a red tray and takes an extra day, which is the honest reason for most delays.

Sorted before 6am, every night except Sunday.

3

Out for delivery, on the day we promised

Your parcel is loaded on a local van in the morning and delivered in a three-hour window. You get a text when it's twenty minutes away. If you're not in, we take it to a neighbour, a locker, or the depot — you choose when you book, and the driver follows it.

Signature only, contactless, or "leave with neighbour" — all free.

4

Delivered, and then the box goes in the cupboard

Our packaging is unbleached cardboard, paper tape and a compostable label. Flatten it, and it goes in your recycling or the council's compost if you have one. No plastic void fill, no branded tape, no plastic document wallets.

A "second-use" bag is £0.40 if you'd like one. Most people don't.

Where the van actually goes

We cover every postcode in England, Wales and Scotland, and both Northern Ireland. The Highlands and Islands take one extra day, and we say so at booking rather than afterwards.

  • EnglandNext day to 94% of postcodes. Cornwall and the far North East are standard at best.
  • ScotlandNext day to Glasgow, Edinburgh and Dundee. Everywhere else, 2–3 days.
  • WalesNext day to the M4 corridor. Anglesey and mid-Wales take 2 days.
  • Northern Ireland2–3 days by ferry. Sunday delivery is not available.

The questions everyone asks

What counts as a large parcel?+

Longest side over 120cm, or more than 60cm of girth — length plus width plus height. Large parcels are £9 more on every service, and we tell you at checkout before you pay, not afterwards.

Do I need to print a label?+

No. If you're booking a collection, bring the parcel out with any old label covered, and the courier prints the new one on the doorstep. If you'd rather print it, we email a PDF that works on a home printer.

What if my parcel is late?+

Standard and economy are covered for late delivery. Next-day parcels that arrive after 10pm on the promised day are refunded in full, automatically, without you having to ask. Late is our problem, not yours.

Can I redirect a parcel?+

Until it's loaded on the van, yes — change the address from your tracking page. After that, ask the driver or leave it with a neighbour, which costs nothing.

Are you a courier company?+

We run our own collection and delivery vans in the South, the South East, the North and Belfast, and we partner with two other carriers for the Highlands and mid-Wales. Tracking, claims and support stay with us either way.
