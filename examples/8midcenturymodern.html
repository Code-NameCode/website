The workshop

We are a small shop with a long memory

Cedar & Stone started in 2009 as a two-person repair bench. Most of what we know came from taking other people's furniture apart and finding out why it failed — the glue joints, the veneer, the one screw holding everything together.

So we design for the repair, not just the photograph. Every joint can be reached, every part can be replaced, and every drawing we publish includes what to use if the part wears out in year twenty.

“A chair that cannot be taken apart is furniture that cannot be kept. That is the whole design brief, and it is why our arm comes off in four bolts.” Ingrid Cedar, founder

How a piece is made

Three stages, ten to fourteen weeks, no shortcuts hidden in the small print

  1. 1

    Timber, chosen

    Walnut and white oak from a single mill in Pennsylvania, air-dried and then kiln-finished to eight per cent. You get the board number on the invoice.

  2. 2

    Built, then dry-fitted

    Joinery first, no glue. The frame is assembled, taken apart, adjusted, and only then glued and pinned. It costs a day and it is the reason the chairs do not rack.

  3. 3

    Finished in oil and wax

    Three coats of hardwax oil, burnished between each. It is a finish you can repair at your own kitchen table in ten minutes, which matters more than a perfect gloss.

Materials

Four materials, chosen to age rather than fail

Nothing in the range is finished with lacquer, and nothing is glued where a screw will do. If a component wears out, you replace that component — not the piece.

Black walnut
American, from a single mill. Colour shifts warm over about five years, then settles. Every board is photographed before it enters the shop.
White oak
Quarter-sawn where the grain shows, rift-sawn where it does not. Used for casework, and for anything that will be near a window.
Saddle leather
Vegetable-tanned, 4mm, from a tannery in Santa Croce that has closed on Sundays since 1961. It creases; it does not crack.
Unlacquered brass
Left raw and allowed to patinate. If you want it bright, the care kit includes the cloth. If you do not touch it for a decade, it goes dark and stays handsome.
