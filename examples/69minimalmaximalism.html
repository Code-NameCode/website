How one kettle is made

  1. Pressed, not cast

    A single steel blank is pressed into body and spout in one operation, which is why there is no seam to fail and no solder anywhere in the product.

    Day 1 — 2
  2. Enamelled twice

    Two coats, fired at 800 °C. The first fills the surface; the second is what you touch. It takes four days and cannot be rushed by adding heat.

    Day 3 — 6
  3. Fitted by one person

    The same person who enamelled it fits the element, the base and the cable, then initials the underside. If a kettle fails in year three, the same workshop answers it.

    Day 7
  4. Numbered and boxed

    Your number is engraved on the base and written on the box in the same hand. There is no barcode, because there is no resale channel to feed.

    Day 8

Why only one

We are a company of four people and we make kettles.

That is the entire business. We have turned down two offers to extend into adjacent categories, one of which was very good money, and we would like to say we did it on principle.

In practice we did it because making a range means making four slightly worse kettles instead of one good one, and nobody at this workshop has the hours. The offer we refused was for a travel flask, and the travel flask would have been fine, and we would have made it badly.

So: one object, one colour, one run. When the 500 are gone we will either make the same kettle again in two years, or we will make something else entirely — and either way it will be a decision made by four people who use a kettle every morning, not by a meeting about a pipeline.
