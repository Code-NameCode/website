How we farm

Three practices, held for a decade.

Regenerative farming is mostly the discipline of not doing things. These are the three we keep.

Permanent pasture, grazed in rotation

Grass stays in the ground for up to eight years, is grazed in short bursts, and is rested until it has flowered again. Roots that survive winter are roots that hold carbon and stop the soil washing into the Medway.

  • Soil tested to 30cm every three years, results public
  • No artificial fertiliser since 2011, including nitrogen
  • Hedge cut on a two-year cycle, never in nesting season
Read the carbon figures →

Direct drilling, no plough at all

The seed goes into last year's residue with a drill, and the field is left alone. The seedbank under the surface builds up for years; the worm population has more than recovered since we stopped inverting the topsoil every autumn.

  • Wheat drilled into clover ley, undersown with vetch
  • Mechanical weed control only — no herbicide since 2016
  • Straw baled and returned to the following crop
See what each field returned →

Water held on the farm

Ponds, ditches and a deliberately leaky corner of ground mean we hold the winter rain where it falls. Less of it races to the Medway as silt, and the flock gets clean water in a dry August without a single tanker.

  • 3.2km of new hedgerow planted since 2016
  • Pond margins left unmown from April for newts
  • Rainwater harvested for washing, no mains on the yard
