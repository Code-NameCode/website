The line

It is one walk, so it is drawn once.

Four stages make a single profile, and the profile is the reason to do it this way rather than as four separate day walks: you sleep high twice, and on the third morning you cross a col where the next stage is out of sight. Hover or focus a day to find it on the line.

DAY 01

Bürglen to Rieder

Eleven kilometres through larch forest and one very long pasture. Nobody is impressed by this stage and everybody's knees notice it by the end. Kit check and briefing at the Bürglen hut at 16:00.

1 120 m → 1 640 m · 11.2 km · 3 h 40 · ↑ 640 m

DAY 02

Rieder to the Moraine Bench

The long one, and the prettiest. Six hours up the Rieder valley with the sun on the left, then a traverse above the treeline to the bench at 2 240 m. The last kilometre is on the flat and everybody finds a reason to take a very long time over it.

1 640 m → 2 240 m · 15.4 km · 6 h 10 · ↑ 1 000 m

DAY 03

Over the Fenner Col

Snow underfoot until about 07:00, hard going on the last 200 metres of ascent, and then the whole northern basin opens at once. This is the only hour of the four days where you cannot see where you are going next.

2 240 m → 2 780 m → 1 980 m · 9.8 km · 4 h 50 · ↑ 540 m

DAY 04

The basin, and out to Kalpetran

Down through larch again, which smells different on the way down. Bus from Kalpetran at 15:10 back to the valley, and a table booked at the inn for whoever wants to stay the extra night.

1 980 m → 1 480 m · 10.1 km · 3 h 20 + bus · ↑ 60 m

Stage three · 2 780 m

The only hour where you cannot see the next stage

Three hours across, and the snow goes hard underfoot at about 07:00. Odette Brunner set the rule in 1974 and it has not been relaxed since: if you are not on the col by 11:00, you go back down the way you came, and nobody says a word about it.

Where you sleep

Three huts, no tents, no hunkering down.

Half board at all three, single rooms at the col, and one night's worth of luggage moved ahead of you by a mule. The bunk fee is already in the price; there is nothing extra to pay on the mountain.

Before you apply

If the weather turns

The third stage is weather-dependent and everybody who has run this route has been stood down at least once. It is worth knowing the rules before you pay a deposit, not after.

Rule one

The 11:00 turnaround

Brunner's rule, unchanged since 1974. Not at the col by 11:00 means back down the way you came, and the group splits if it has to. You pay nothing extra and nobody treats it as a failure.

Rule two

What a stand-down costs

Nothing. If a stage is cancelled we move you to the other week at no cost, or refund the deposit in full within ten days. Huts are paid for either way, because the beds are held from the first booking of the season.

Rule three

Insurance, honestly

We require verified cover to at least 3 000 m that includes helicopter rescue — most travel policies do not, and the two that do are named on the application form. If you cannot get cover, tell us before you book and we will say whether the route suits.
