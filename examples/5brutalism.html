Issue 31 · Winter 2026 · Printed by hand in a shed in Rochdale

We print on a machine that was old when the Queen was on the throne.

RAW PRESS is a 48-page zine about printing, publishing, ink and the small arguments that happen in copy meetings. Two colours. One pass. No digital layout — the type is set by hand on a composing stick and locked into a chase, which takes most people six hours and us four.

We do not run a website shop, we do not do pre-orders, and we do not reprint. When a run of 400 is gone it is gone, and the people who got one will have to argue about it on the internet like everybody else.

What we believe

Six positions, printed on the inside cover since Issue 1

  1. 01

    A run is a decision4,000 copies is not success, it is 3,600 in a landfill. We print what we think we can sell and lose money doing it.

  2. 02

    The reader is not a metricNo newsletter, no “community”, no engagement. You either want the thing or you do not.

  3. 03

    Two colours is a disciplineEvery extra ink is a decision you have to defend. We have never printed more than two and we never will.

  4. 04

    Corrections are printedIn the next issue, not quietly fixed. If we got it wrong, you get to read about it.

  5. 05

    Slow is not a virtueSix weeks is as long as we can justify. Six months is a lie we tell ourselves about craft.

  6. 06

    Paper is the messageWe do not laminate, we do not foil, we do not put a spine on it. Newsprint, because newsprint.

Submit to us

We answer everything. We print about one thing in five.

Write to us properly. We are not looking for pitches that could be anything; we are looking for arguments about printing that only you could make.

  1. Read three back issues first. They are in every decent second-hand bookshop and we will not apologise for the price.
  2. Write 1,200 to 3,000 words. If it needs to be 8,000 it is a book and we are not a publisher.
  3. Send a plain-text file or a clean Markdown export. Not a PDF. Never a PDF.
  4. Include one thing you got wrong in public and how you found out. This raises your chances considerably.
  5. We reply within three weeks either way. “No” comes with a reason. “Yes” comes with a page and a date.
